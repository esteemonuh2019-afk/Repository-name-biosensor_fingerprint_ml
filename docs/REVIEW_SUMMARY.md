## Project Objective

Develop a machine learning platform capable of identifying environmental contaminants using temporal luminescence fingerprints generated by engineered whole-cell biosensors.

---

# Biological Question

Can contaminant-specific luminescence fingerprints generated by bacterial whole-cell biosensors be used to reliably identify environmental contaminants across independent experiments?

---

# Key Scientific Findings

## Finding 1

Random train/test performance substantially overestimated real-world performance.

Observed:

* Classification performance ≈ 98%

LOEO validation demonstrated lower experiment-independent performance.

---

## Finding 2

BL027 emerged as the strongest overall biosensor strain.

---

## Finding 3

Advanced temporal feature engineering improved experiment-independent classification.

---

## Finding 4

Different contaminants exhibited strain-specific recognition patterns.

Examples:

* BL027 strongly recognized Boric Acid.
* BL029 strongly recognized Diazinon.
* BL032 strongly recognized Trimethoprim.

---

## Finding 5

Universal biosensor architectures may not be optimal.

Evidence suggests contaminant-specific biosensor specialization.
